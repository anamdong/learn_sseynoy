Sseynoy is a constructed Sinitic language with analytic grammar, lexical tone, classifiers, and a Latin-script spelling system. Everyday speech uses colloquial forms, while Sino-derived vocabulary is especially visible in formal and literary language.

Overview

A typical clause places the subject before the verb and object. Soi hàw je. means “I like you.” Verbs do not change to agree with person or number in the examples available today. Time, completion, negation, and viewpoint are expressed with separate words and particles.

The modern first- and second-person forms are soi and je. Their common possessives are sek and jek. Plural forms add -dáy: soidáy, jedáy, and ydáy.

Sound and writing

Sseynoy marks rising tone with an acute accent and falling tone with a grave accent. An unmarked syllable has level tone. Final stops form a checked class. Accents matter: the benefactive word vì has falling tone.

The spelling distinguishes several vowel and glide patterns. oi in soi is /oj/, while oy in coy has a front-rounded offglide. ey is a long vowel /iː/, as in gwey; it is not a diphthong. The spellings au and aw both represent /au/.

Grammar

Nouns, possession, and classifiers

Possessors come before nouns: sek ga is “my house,” ydek shuh is “his or her book,” and John gàa ga is “John’s house.” Classifiers appear between numbers and nouns, as in it-gueon shuh or it-gàa shuh, “a book.”

Aspect and negation

yí marks completion in many examples, mèy means “not yet,” and zung … -gwa describes previous experience. Negation often uses fue. A few contracted negatives are established, including zhi'ng “not know” and oe'ng shik “not be eating”; contraction cannot be freely added to every verb.

Questions and relative clauses

keo marks questions in examples such as Je oe xàng kachúh keo?, “Where are you going?” Relative clauses come before the noun they describe: Je só máay shé shuh means “the book that you bought.”

Useful patterns

Ca shuh, soi gé duek yí.As for this book, I have already read it.

Moving an object to the beginning can make it the topic. For placement, both Soi zàang shuh fàng oe zhak sàng. and ordinary Soi fàng shuh oe zhak sàng. are available.

Y hòen zỳgí.He hates himself.
Ydáy sang ày.They love each other.
Soi vì je máay ca yí.I bought this for you.

Mun shet introduces reported information: Mun shet, y gé li yí., “Apparently, he has already left.” Repetition can also shape an action: Kàn kàn. invites someone to have a look, while Zàey kàn zàey kàn. means to look again and again.

The position of fue controls what is negated:

Soi fue yok máy gàa rin lay.I don’t want everyone to come.
Soi yok máy gàa rin fue lay.I want everyone not to come.

Vocabulary and characters

Sseynoy has colloquial vocabulary and a large set of character readings. A character’s Sino-derived reading does not automatically replace the everyday word used in a sentence. For example, the colloquial pronouns are soi and je, even when a character lookup shows a different inherited reading.

The 25 November update redistributes many Sino readings by individual character. The learning site applies these changes only when both the character and its earlier tone-neutral reading match. Updated results are shown as tone-neutral where a tone has not been established.
